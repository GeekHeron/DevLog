03 / How I Do Enterprise AI

One approach, two threads: the organization decides what to do, the technology decides how to do it.

Thread 1 · Organizational productivity methodology

I don't start from models, but from business pain points. Split a role into human/AI tasks, turn individual experience into organizational knowledge, and verify value with business results.

Find scenarios→Split roles→Build knowledge→Run workflows→Iterate
Find scenarios · A wrong start ruins everything — answer "what business problem are we solving" first, then screen by frequency, repetition, data, and value.
Split roles · AI doesn't replace a job, it absorbs the low-value mental work inside it — break the role into tasks, then split them between people and AI.
Build knowledge · Don't jump to LangChain — turn experience into organizational assets, build the data capability layer and business ontology before Agents.
Run workflows · ERP remembers, AI understands, Agents act — get AI into end-to-end business flows, not just a chat window.
Iterate · Success is business results, not a launched system — verify with ROI and operating data, then optimize the next round.
Excerpts from the blog: role splitting · data capability layer · AI ERP · RAG pitfalls

04 / Common Questions

Where does a project start and how long does it take?+

Typically a 90-day pilot: diagnose and select scenarios, prepare the organization and knowledge, run one to three real scenarios, then evaluate and replicate. The goal is not company-wide AI from day one. Full plan in Enterprise Productivity Methodology.

How is the effort estimated and value measured?+

Based on system count, data complexity, and delivery scope. Track four layers — efficiency, quality, business, and financial — and verify with ROI. Calculation details in Private LLM Cost Breakdown.

Who from the enterprise needs to be involved?+

Business scenario owners provide requirements and acceptance; knowledge and process owners maintain standards; tech and data teams handle systems and security; leadership owns goals and resources — it is not just an IT project. See Enterprise Productivity Methodology.
